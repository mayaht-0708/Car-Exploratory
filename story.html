Story

Three stories from the vehicle data

Each story asks a different question about price, vehicle size, performance, and fuel economy. The findings below use cleaned vehicle records; the About the Data page explains the source and cleaning steps.

Story 1: Is More Horsepower Worth the Price?

Across 11,129 cleaned vehicle configurations, MSRP and horsepower have a positive correlation of 0.66. Higher-horsepower vehicles tend to cost more, but the relationship is not absolute.

Coral points have below-median MSRP ($30,600) and above-median horsepower (239 hp). Examples among 2010-and-newer vehicles include:

  • Toyota Tundra (2015): 381 hp at $29,120.
  • Hyundai Genesis Coupe (2015): 348 hp at $26,750.
  • Nissan 370Z (2015): 332 hp at $29,990.

Price alone does not guarantee more horsepower: 797 of 5,560 configurations priced above the median have median-or-lower horsepower. Horsepower is only one part of performance.

Story 2: Do Bigger Cars Use More Gas?

In the cleaned data, average city and highway MPG generally decline from compact to midsize to large vehicles. For example, compact vehicles average 21.7 city / 28.6 highway MPG, compared with 15.6 city / 22 highway MPG for large vehicles.

There are exceptions. A 2014 Infiniti Q70, classified as large, is listed at 29 city / 34 highway MPG—higher than the compact-size averages. Size is a useful broad pattern, not a rule for every model. Electric vehicles use MPG-equivalent (MPGe) ratings in these source fields, so the chart compares reported efficiency rather than literal gallons for every car.

Story 3: The Best Balance Between Performance and Fuel Efficiency

For this comparison, “high performance and good fuel economy” means above the cleaned dataset medians: 239 hp, 18 city MPG, and 25 highway MPG. Vehicles that clear all three thresholds include:

  • Infiniti Q50 (2015): 360 hp, 29 city / 36 highway MPG.
  • BMW 5 Series (2015): 255 hp, 26 city / 38 highway MPG.
  • Volvo S60 (2016): 240 hp, 26 city / 38 highway MPG.

These are examples, not universal recommendations: they stand out because their horsepower and both MPG ratings exceed the dataset medians. Their prices, vehicle sizes, and ownership costs differ, and the dataset does not measure reliability, acceleration, or what buyers actually pay. Compare individual models on the Exploration tab before drawing conclusions.
